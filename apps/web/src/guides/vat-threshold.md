## The short answer

You must [register for VAT](https://www.gov.uk/vat-registration) if either of these is true:

- Your VAT taxable turnover for **the last 12 months** went over **£90,000**.
- You expect your turnover in **the next 30 days alone** to go over £90,000.

The threshold has been £90,000 since April 2024, and the Budget on 28 October 2026 didn't change it.

## It's turnover, not profit

This catches a lot of people out. Turnover is **everything you invoice** (labour and materials), not what's left after costs.

> You invoice £60,000 of labour and £35,000 of materials you passed on to customers in a year. Your turnover is £95,000, so you're over the threshold, even if your profit is much smaller.

Nearly all building and trade work counts. That includes work that's zero-rated, like building a new home.

## The rolling 12 months

The 12 months isn't your tax year or your accounts year. It's **any 12 months in a row**. At the end of every month, add up the last 12 months.

If you go over:

1. Tell HMRC by the **end of the following month**.
2. You're VAT registered from the **first day of the month after that**.

> Your 12-month total goes over £90,000 at the end of May 2026. You must register by 30 June 2026, and you're registered from 1 July 2026. From then on, you charge VAT on your invoices.

## The 30-day test

If you win a big contract and know that in the next 30 days alone you'll invoice more than £90,000, you must register **straight away**, not at the end of the month.

## If you miss it

If you register late, you owe HMRC the VAT from the date you _should_ have registered, even though you never charged your customers for it. That usually comes out of your own pocket, and HMRC can add a penalty. Keeping an eye on your 12-month total is much cheaper.

## Should you register before you have to?

You can register at any time, even well under £90,000. Whether it's worth it depends on who your customers are:

|                                             | Mostly business customers           | Mostly homeowners                                                         |
| ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Adding 20% VAT to your prices               | They reclaim it, so they don't mind | They can't reclaim it, so your prices go up 20%, or your profit goes down |
| Reclaiming VAT on tools, vans and materials | Yes                                 | Yes                                                                       |
| Usually worth registering early?            | Often                               | Usually not                                                               |

There's also more paperwork: VAT returns every quarter, kept digitally under Making Tax Digital.

## Construction has extra VAT rules

Building work has some special rules worth asking an accountant about, such as the **domestic reverse charge** (for VAT-registered subcontractors working for contractors in the Construction Industry Scheme), and lower rates for some work, like building new homes and installing some energy-saving materials.

## Coming off VAT

If your taxable turnover for the next 12 months is expected to be **£88,000 or less**, you can ask HMRC to cancel your registration.

## Keep an eye on your total

The easiest way to avoid a surprise is to check your monthly income regularly. TradeReports shows your income by month and by VAT quarter from your invoices export, so adding up the last 12 months takes seconds.
