Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax changes **how** sole traders report to HMRC, not **how much** tax they pay. Instead of one tax return a year, you keep your records in software and send HMRC a short update every three months.

## Does it apply to you?

It applies to **sole traders and landlords**. It doesn't apply to limited companies, and partnerships aren't included yet.

What counts is your **qualifying income**: your total turnover from self-employment and property before any expenses, as shown on your tax return. Wages from a job don't count.

| You must start from | If your qualifying income was over | On your tax return for |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| 6 April 2026        | £50,000                            | 2024 to 2025           |
| 6 April 2027        | £30,000                            | 2025 to 2026           |
| 6 April 2028        | £20,000                            | 2026 to 2027           |

> A plumber who turned over £38,000 in the 2025 to 2026 tax year (before costs for parts, van and fuel) will need to use Making Tax Digital from 6 April 2027.

## What changes

1. **Digital records.** You keep a record of your income and expenses in software that works with HMRC. A spreadsheet can still be used if you link it to HMRC with "bridging" software.
2. **Quarterly updates.** Every three months, your software sends HMRC a summary of your income and expenses.
3. **A final declaration** by 31 January replaces the old tax return.

The quarterly deadlines are the same every year:

| Quarter                | Send it by |
| ---------------------- | ---------- |
| 6 April to 5 July      | 7 August   |
| 6 July to 5 October    | 7 November |
| 6 October to 5 January | 7 February |
| 6 January to 5 April   | 7 May      |

## What doesn't change

- It isn't a new tax. You report the same income and expenses as before.
- The dates you **pay** your tax stay the same.
- You can still use an accountant, who can send the updates for you.

## What to do now

- **Check your last tax return** and add up your turnover from self-employment and property. That tells you which date applies to you.
- **Choose software** that works with Making Tax Digital. Ask your accountant first if you have one; many already use a package that does.
- **Record as you go.** Put every invoice and receipt in as it happens, rather than in a box until January. Your quarterly updates then take minutes.
- **Use a separate bank account** for the business if you don't already. It makes the records far easier to keep.

If your turnover is under £20,000, you don't have to join yet, but keeping digital records now will make the switch painless later.

## Where TradeReports fits in

TradeReports isn't Making Tax Digital software, and it doesn't send anything to HMRC. What it does do is turn your invoices export into income by month and by quarter in seconds, which is handy for checking your numbers before an update goes in.
