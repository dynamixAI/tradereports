A good invoice does two jobs: it keeps you on the right side of HMRC, and it makes it easy for the customer to pay you. Here's what has to be on it, and what's worth adding.

## What every invoice must show

According to [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/invoicing-and-taking-payment-from-customers/invoices-what-they-must-include), every invoice must include:

- a **unique invoice number**
- **your** business name, address and contact details
- the **customer's** name and address
- a clear **description of the work** you're charging for
- the **date the work was done**
- the **date of the invoice**
- the **amount** for each item
- the **VAT**, if you charge it
- the **total** owed

## Extra details by type of business

**Sole traders** must also show your own name as well as any business name you use. If you trade under a business name, include an address where legal documents can be delivered to you.

**Limited companies** must show the full company name exactly as it is on the certificate of incorporation. "Bradley Joinery" isn't enough if the company is "Bradley Joinery Ltd". If you put any directors' names on the invoice, you have to name all of them.

**VAT-registered businesses** need a full VAT invoice, which adds:

- your **VAT registration number**
- the **tax point** (usually the date the work was finished)
- the **VAT rate** and the **VAT amount** shown separately from the price

If your customer is also VAT registered, you must give them a VAT invoice, and you should send it within 30 days of finishing the work.

## Not required, but gets you paid faster

These aren't legal requirements, but they're often the difference between being paid this week and next month:

- **A due date.** "Payment due by 8 December 2026" is clearer than "30 days".
- **How to pay.** Bank name, sort code, account number, and the reference you want them to use.
- **The job address**, if it's different from the billing address. Landlords and letting agents with lots of properties need it.
- **Their order number**, for business customers. Many won't pay an invoice without one.
- **A late payment line** for business customers, such as: "Late payments may be charged interest under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998." See [can you charge interest on a late invoice?](/guides/late-payment-interest)

## Numbering your invoices

Every invoice needs its own number, and they should run in order (0001, 0002, 0003 or 2026-041, 2026-042). Never reuse a number. If you make a mistake, send a credit note for the old invoice and issue a new one, rather than editing an invoice you've already sent.

## Common mistakes

- **Wrong bank details**, so the customer can't pay you even if they want to.
- **Vague descriptions** like "work as agreed", which invite questions and delay payment.
- **Invoicing the wrong person**, such as a tenant instead of the landlord who ordered the work.
- **Showing VAT when you're not registered.** You can only charge VAT once you're registered.
- **Waiting weeks to invoice.** Send it the day you finish, while the customer is happy with the job.

## Keep a copy

Keep a copy of every invoice you send. If you're a sole trader, HMRC expects you to keep your records for at least 5 years after the 31 January deadline for that tax year. Limited companies usually need to keep records for 6 years.

Keeping them in your job software is fine, and its export is exactly what [TradeReports](/app) uses to show you who owes you and how late they are.
