It's tempting. Export your invoices, drop the file into an AI chatbot and ask "who owes me money?" A few seconds later you've got an answer. But before you do, it's worth knowing where that file goes, who can see it, and what the law expects of you, because your invoices aren't just your data. They're your customers' too.

## Where your file actually goes

AI chatbots don't run on your computer. When you paste text or upload a file, it's sent over the internet to the AI company's servers, processed there, and usually **kept there**, at least for a while.

What happens next depends on the company, the plan you're on, and your settings:

- **Personal ChatGPT accounts:** OpenAI can use your conversations, including files you upload, to train its AI unless you switch it off. The setting is under **Settings → Data Controls → "Improve the model for everyone"**. Business plans and OpenAI's developer service don't do this by default.
- **Google's free Gemini developer service:** Google's terms say that on the free tier it uses what you send to improve its products, and that human reviewers may read it.
- **Other AI tools:** the rules vary from company to company and change over time. Many small tools are built on top of the big AI companies, so your data may pass through more than one business.

None of this means the companies are doing anything wrong. It's all in their terms. But most people never read them, and the defaults aren't always the private option.

## Your invoices are full of other people's details

A typical invoices export holds your customers' **names, home addresses, phone numbers and email addresses**, what work you did in their homes, and how much they paid. Under UK data protection law (UK GDPR), that's personal data, and **you're responsible for it**.

The Information Commissioner's Office (ICO), the UK's data protection regulator, is clear that text and files entered into AI tools can be personal data, and that the usual rules apply. In practice, if you upload customer details to an AI tool, you should:

- have a good reason (a "lawful basis") for doing it
- tell your customers, in your privacy notice, that their details may go to AI tools
- use a service with a proper **data processing agreement**, which business plans usually offer and free personal accounts don't
- know where the data is stored, and for how long

Most sole traders don't have any of that in place. That's not a criticism; the rules were written long before anyone uploaded a spreadsheet to a chatbot. But it does mean "just paste it in" carries more risk than it looks.

## AI is great with words, bad with sums

There's a second problem: **AI chatbots can get numbers wrong**, and sound completely sure while doing it. They're built to write convincing text, not to add up columns. An answer like "you're owed £14,320" might be right, or it might be a confident guess. With your money, a wrong total is worse than no total at all.

## If you do use AI, use it safely

AI can still be genuinely useful for a trade business. A few simple rules keep you on the right side:

1. **Never paste customer names, addresses or phone numbers** into a personal AI account.
2. **Ask general questions, not questions about your data.** "How do I word a polite payment reminder?" is fine. "Here's my customer list…" isn't.
3. **Remove personal details first** if you must share figures. Totals by month are fine; a list of who owes what is not.
4. **Use a business plan** with a data processing agreement if you want to use AI with customer data regularly, and **turn off training** in the settings.
5. **Check any figures** an AI gives you against your own records.

## Questions to ask any tool before you upload

Whether it's an AI chatbot, an app or a website, ask:

- **Does my file leave my computer?** If so, where does it go, and which country is it stored in?
- **How long is it kept?** Is it deleted when I'm done?
- **Is it used to train AI?** Is that on by default, and can I switch it off?
- **Can people at the company read it?**
- **Is there a data processing agreement** I can rely on for my customers' details?

If the answers are hard to find, that tells you something too.
