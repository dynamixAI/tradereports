You did the job, sent the invoice, and the due date has come and gone. It happens to every trade. Most late payers aren't trying to dodge you: the invoice got buried, they forgot, or money is tight this month. A clear, polite system gets most of them to pay without falling out.

## First, check your own invoice

Before you chase, make sure nothing on your side is holding things up:

- **Is it the right person?** The name and address of whoever agreed the work, not just whoever let you in.
- **Is the due date clear?** "Payment within 14 days" or a date, not "on completion".
- **Are your bank details right?** One wrong digit and they can't pay you even if they want to.
- **Did it arrive?** Emails land in spam. A quick "just checking you got the invoice" message is often all it takes.

## A simple chasing timeline

| When                   | What to send                       | How              |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| Day after the due date | Friendly reminder                  | Text or email    |
| 7 days late            | Second reminder, then a phone call | Email and phone  |
| 14 days late           | Firm reminder                      | Email or letter  |
| 30 days late           | Final notice                       | Letter and email |

Keep a note of every message and call: the date, what you said and what they said. If it ever goes further, that record matters.

## Messages you can copy

Change the bits in square brackets. Keep it short and polite every time: being firm is about being clear, not angry.

### 1. Friendly reminder (the day after it's due)

> Hi [name], just a quick reminder that invoice [number] for [the job] (£[amount]) was due yesterday. In case it's easier, my bank details are [sort code / account number], reference [invoice number]. Thanks, [your name]

### 2. Second reminder (a week late)

> Hi [name], I haven't yet received payment for invoice [number] (£[amount]), which was due on [date]. Could you let me know when I can expect it? If there's a problem with the work or the invoice, tell me and I'll sort it. Thanks, [your name]

Follow this one with a phone call. People who ignore emails often answer the phone, and a call is the quickest way to find out if there's a real problem.

### 3. Firm reminder (two weeks late)

> Dear [name], invoice [number] for £[amount] is now 14 days overdue. Please pay it by [date, 7 days from now]. [For business customers:] Under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 I'm entitled to charge interest and a fixed late payment fee on overdue invoices, and I'll add these if payment isn't received by that date. Kind regards, [your name]

Only mention interest to business customers, or to homeowners whose agreed terms included it. See [can you charge interest on a late invoice?](/guides/late-payment-interest)

### 4. Final notice (a month late)

> Dear [name], despite my reminders of [dates], invoice [number] for £[amount] remains unpaid. Please pay in full by [date]. If I don't receive payment or hear from you by then, I'll start formal steps to recover the money, which may include court action. Yours sincerely, [your name]

## If they still don't pay

The next steps depend on who owes you.

- **A homeowner, or another sole trader:** in England and Wales you must follow the [Pre-Action Protocol for Debt Claims](https://www.justice.gov.uk/courts/procedure-rules/civil/pdf/protocols/debt-pap.pdf) before going to court. That means sending a formal _letter of claim_ (with the forms the protocol lists) and waiting **30 days** for a reply.
- **A limited company:** send a _letter before action_ giving a reasonable time to pay, usually **14 days**.
- **Small claims court:** in England and Wales you can [make a court claim for money](https://www.gov.uk/make-court-claim-for-money) online for debts up to £10,000, without a solicitor. There's a court fee, which depends on the amount. Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own systems.
- **A large company paying you late:** the [Small Business Commissioner](https://www.smallbusinesscommissioner.gov.uk/) may be able to help.

Often the letter alone does it: once it's formal, most people find the money.

## Stop it happening next time

- **Agree payment terms before you start.** Put them on the quote: "Payment due within 14 days of the invoice."
- **Take a deposit or stage payments on bigger jobs**, so you're never carrying the whole cost.
- **Invoice the day you finish**, while the job is fresh and the customer is happy.
- **Make paying easy:** bank details and a reference on every invoice, or a card payment link.
- **Know who owes you, every week.** It's hard to chase what you can't see.

_This guide is general information, not legal advice. Rules differ in Scotland and Northern Ireland. Checked against GOV.UK and the Civil Procedure Rules in November 2026._
